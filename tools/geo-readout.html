What this assumes, and what it does not cover
  • The same model the test was designed with. Markets are the units and each market's pre-period is a covariate: an ordinary least squares regression of test-period revenue on the group and the pre-period (analysis of covariance). It is what the geo holdout page assumes when it adjusts the variation for the pre-period correlation. The effect per test market, times the number of test markets, is the incremental revenue. The interval uses the classical standard error and a t distribution with three fewer degrees of freedom than markets.
  • It is not the best method available. GeoLift (synthetic control) and Google's time-based regression use each market's full daily history and will usually give a tighter, better-calibrated answer from the same test. This page reads the test from two totals per market. That makes it quick, and it leaves out the daily history those tools use.
  • Big markets are noisier. The classical standard error treats every market's noise as the same size, and revenue does not behave that way. The published comparison shows the robust (HC1) error and the weighted regression of Vaver & Koehler (2011) next to this one, on the same data. On its five data sets the weighted estimate lands anywhere from 19% above this one to 69% below it, inside this page's interval every time.
  • Spillover pushes toward zero. People travel, word of mouth crosses borders, and IP-based geo targeting is commonly cited as only 55–80% accurate, so some held-out markets saw the ads anyway.
  • The spend is taken as known. The iROAS interval is the revenue interval divided by the spend. The iROAS counts revenue before margin: at 1, the campaign paid for itself before margin, not after.
  • What a conversion is worth.
  • The MMM prior is unadjusted. Its mean is the iROAS and its standard deviation is the iROAS's standard error. Meridian's calibration builder also adjusts for how long the test ran, how its spend compares with the channel's and how old it is. This page makes none of those adjustments. Robyn keeps only the point estimate. Both models take the test's weeks and markets as standing for the channel as a whole.
  • The lift in the test markets has no interval. It is the incremental revenue over what the test markets would have made without the campaign, and that baseline is estimated too, so it is shown as an estimate only.
  • Not covered: daily or weekly series, staggered starts, more than two groups, adstock that runs past the test window, and markets assigned by anything other than chance.
