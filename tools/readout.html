What this assumes, and what it does not cover
  • The traffic split is checked first. A chi-square test of the users in each arm against the split you planned, with no continuity correction. Below p = 0.001 the page does not read the lift at all. Eppo and GrowthBook use the same threshold; Microsoft's experimentation team uses a stricter 0.0005, and Lukas Vermeer's retired checker a looser 0.01.
  • Normal approximation. The p-value is the standard two-proportion z-test with the variance pooled under the null, the same number a chi-square test on the 2×2 table gives. With fewer than 10 conversions (or non-conversions) in an arm it stops being reliable, and the page warns you.
  • The interval for the difference is Newcombe's, built from the two Wilson intervals. It keeps its coverage when a rate is near zero or an arm is small, where the textbook Wald interval does not. At large samples the two agree to the second decimal.
  • The interval for the relative lift comes from the log of the ratio of the two rates, so it is not symmetric around the lift, and it needs at least one conversion in each arm.
  • The exaggeration ratio (type M error) is Gelman and Carlin's: if the true lift were the one you planned for, how much a significant estimate would overstate it on average. It uses your planned lift on the observed control rate and the standard error from your data, never the lift you observed. Power computed from the observed lift is a restatement of the p-value and tells you nothing new.
  • Two-sided only, one metric, one look. Checking the result along the way (see the peeking checker) or choosing the best of several metrics afterwards makes every number here look better than it is.
  • Not covered: revenue and other continuous metrics, more than two variants, sequential or always-valid designs, CUPED-adjusted estimates, and users who are not independent of each other.

Cross-checked against statsmodels (proportions_ztest, confint_proportions_2indep), scipy.stats.chisquare, and a Monte Carlo for the exaggeration ratio. See the published comparison.
