What this assumes, and what it does not cover
  • Normal approximation to the binomial. Standard for this calculation and accurate at realistic sample sizes, but it degrades when the expected number of conversions per variant drops into single digits. The tool warns you when that happens.
  • Pooled variance under the null. The null standard error is computed at the average of the two rates. Evan Miller's calculator uses the baseline rate in both arms instead and returns about 6% fewer users. Both conventions are defensible, and the comparison is published.
  • No continuity correction. Adding one (the Fleiss convention) raises the requirement by roughly 4%.
  • An even 50/50 split and equal traffic to both variants for the whole run.
  • One primary metric, analysed once. Every extra metric you check is another chance at a false positive, and so is every extra look at the same metric.
  • Not covered: revenue and other continuous metrics, more than two variants, sequential or always-valid designs, and any correction for the fact that users are not always independent.
