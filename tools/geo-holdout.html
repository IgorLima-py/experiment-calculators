What this assumes, and what it does not cover
  • Markets are independent. They are not, quite. People travel, word of mouth crosses borders, and IP-based geo targeting is commonly cited as only 55–80% accurate, so some of your dark markets see the ads anyway. Every one of those effects drags the measured lift toward zero, which means a real effect looks smaller than it is.
  • The pre-period was stable. One regional promotion, one storm, one competitor launch in either group and the comparison is measuring that instead.
  • Market-level metrics are skewed. Raw DMA revenue is heavy-tailed — a handful of markets dominate. That is why the honest input is the variation of a pre-period-adjusted metric, not of raw totals, and why one whale market can still distort the result. Stratify by size when you assign groups.
  • This is a design-stage estimate. It is the standard first-order method (a two-sample t-test with markets as units, in the Hayes & Bennett cluster-trial tradition), and it deliberately asks less of you than the tools that do this properly. GeoLift and Trimmed Match fit a synthetic control and simulate on your actual market history, and will usually squeeze a better minimum detectable effect out of the same markets. Run one of them before you commit budget.
  • No delayed effects. If your channel has an adstock tail, the test window has to cover it or you will measure a fraction of the true effect.
  • Not covered: synthetic control and matched-market designs, unequal market sizes as an explicit weight, staggered rollouts, and any channel you cannot actually split geographically — SEO, podcast, PR.

Power uses the noncentral t distribution, checked against scipy.stats.nct and statsmodels TTestIndPower — see the published comparison.
